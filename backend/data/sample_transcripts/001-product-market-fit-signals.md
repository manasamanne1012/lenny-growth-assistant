# How you actually know you have product-market fit

**Lenny:** Everyone quotes the Marc Andreessen line about feeling it. That is not
very actionable for a founder at 2am. What do you actually look at?

**Priya Raman:** [00:02:14] I look at one thing before anything else: whether
retention flattens. If your month-six cohort curve is still sloping down, you do
not have product-market fit, no matter what your top-line growth says. Growth can
be bought. A flat retention curve cannot.

**Priya Raman:** [00:04:30] The second signal is qualitative and people
underweight it. When you have it, the work changes shape. You stop convincing
people to try the thing and you start apologising for how slow it is. Support
volume goes up and it is all about capacity, not confusion. That transition is
unmistakable once you have lived through it.

**Lenny:** What about the surveys — the "how disappointed would you be" question?

**Priya Raman:** [00:07:02] The forty percent benchmark is a useful smoke alarm
and a terrible thermostat. It tells you something is happening. It will not tell
you what to build next. I have seen teams hit forty-two percent and optimise
themselves straight into a niche they could not grow out of. Use it to decide
whether to keep going, never to decide what to do.

**Priya Raman:** [00:09:48] The mistake I see most often is treating
product-market fit as a state you arrive at. It is a relationship between a
product and a segment at a moment. Change the segment and you are back at zero.
We learned that expensively when we moved upmarket — the same product, the same
team, and the retention curve fell over again.
