# Adding sales to a product-led company without breaking it

**Dana Okoye:** [00:02:00] The first thing to accept is that product-led growth
and a sales motion are not a spectrum you slide along. They are two operating
systems with different definitions of a good week. Self-serve optimises for
removing humans. Sales optimises for inserting the right human at the right
moment. Run both badly and you get a demo request form bolted onto a free trial.

**Lenny:** When is it time to add sales?

**Dana Okoye:** [00:04:45] When accounts start hitting a ceiling they cannot
cross alone. Watch for expansion that stalls at a consistent seat count. That is
usually procurement, security review, or a workflow that needs someone senior to
sign off. None of those are solvable with better empty states.

**Dana Okoye:** [00:07:20] The pricing trap is real. The self-serve tier is
priced on value to an individual; the enterprise tier gets priced on value to an
organisation. If you do not build a deliberate bridge between them, your sales
team spends its time competing with your own free tier and losing.

**Dana Okoye:** [00:10:10] We also underestimated the internal cost. Product
suddenly had two customers with opposite requests. Self-serve users wanted fewer
settings; enterprise buyers wanted every setting to be an admin policy. We
resolved it by making the org-level controls invisible to individual users by
default, which sounds obvious and took us four months to agree on.

**Dana Okoye:** [00:13:05] If I were doing it again I would hire one salesperson
and give them a quota that only counts expansion inside existing accounts. New
logos through a sales team, while your funnel is still product-led, is how you
end up with two companies in one building.
